    Choose float64 or float32¶

    ace-jax evaluates models in float64 or float32. float64 is the default and the reference. float32 can be faster, but it adds round-off error to energies and forces. This page tells you when float32 is faster, how to measure its error for your model, and which tasks can use it.

    Fitting and radial learning always use float64.

    Enable float64 in Python

    Fitting and radial learning need float64. The aj command enables float64 itself. In Python, enable float64 before any other code imports JAX:

    import jax
jax.config.update("jax_enable_x64", True)

    Alternatively, set JAX_ENABLE_X64=1 in the environment. ace-jax never changes the JAX precision setting itself. The JAX default is float32.

    When float32 is faster¶

    The speed gain depends on the float64 throughput of the GPU.

    GPU float32 speed-up (silicon, LAMMPS, 32768 atoms)
    RTX A4500 (low float64 throughput) 2.1 to 4.5 times
    A100 (full float64 throughput) 1.3 to 1.5 times

    Most workstation GPUs (RTX, L40) have low float64 throughput, as the RTX A4500 does. The gain is largest for small models and smallest for large models. On an A100, float64 is usually the better choice.

    Measure the float32 error of your model¶

    The float32 error depends on the model more than on the code. Large coefficients that cancel make it larger. Thus, measure it for each model.

    1. Enable float64 (see above).
    2. Make structures that represent your task. Include the most distorted structures that the task visits, for example hot MD, defect cores or transition states.

    3. Compare float32 with float64 on each structure:

      import ace_jax as aj
from ace_jax.calc.precision import precision_check

model, meta, _ = aj.load("model.npz")
check = precision_check(model, meta, atoms)
print(check["dE_meV_atom"], check["dF_rel"], check["dF_max_meV_A"])

    4. Record the energy error per atom (dE_meV_atom), the relative force error (dF_rel) and the maximum force error (dF_max_meV_A).

    aj export --dtype float32 --structure start.xyz does the same check on the initial structure of a LAMMPS run. A warning occurs above 0.1 meV/atom or a relative force error of 1e-4.

    These are typical values for silicon models (linear ACE, order 4), from small to large:

    error small model (degree 12 to 14) large model (degree 16 to 20)
    maximum force error 0.5 to 1.0 meV/Å 0.07 to 0.35 meV/Å
    relative force error 2e-4 to 4e-4 2e-5 to 1e-4
    energy error 0.12 to 0.21 meV/atom 0.004 to 0.02 meV/atom

    The errors on strongly distorted structures can be 5 to 25 times larger than these values.

    Tasks that can use float32¶

    float32 is safe when the quantity that you need is much larger than the float32 error. Tasks that use forces are more tolerant than tasks that compare small energy differences.

    task float32
    MD with a thermostat: averages, radial distribution functions, diffusion safe
    NVE MD at a timestep of 1 fs or less safe
    geometry optimisation to a maximum force of approximately 3e-3 eV/Å or more, with a force-only optimiser safe
    properties of relaxed structures: elastic constants, defect and surface energies safe for well-conditioned models; measure first
    geometry optimisation to a maximum force of 1e-3 eV/Å or less; tight NEB or dimer searches caution
    NVE MD at 2 fs over long times caution
    small energy differences between large cells caution
    optimisers with an energy line search (L-BFGS or CG with Armijo or Wolfe tests) do not use
    finite differences of energies, free energy perturbation, thermodynamic integration do not use without a float64 check

    Geometry optimisation¶

    Energy line searches fail in float32

    The float32 energy noise is larger than the energy change along a short line search step. The Armijo and Wolfe tests then fail. Use an optimiser that uses only forces, for example ASE ODE12r.

    import numpy as np
import ace_jax as aj
from ase.optimize import ODE12r

atoms.calc = aj.ACECalculator("model.npz", dtype=np.float32)
ODE12r(atoms).run(fmax=3e-3)

    The optimiser cannot go below the float32 force error. Set fmax to approximately 3 times the maximum force error of your model, or larger.

    Molecular dynamics¶

    In NVE MD of silicon (4096 and 32768 atoms, 10 to 40 ps, LAMMPS), float32 did not increase the energy drift at timesteps of 0.5 fs and 1 fs. It added noise of approximately 0.01 meV/atom to the total energy. This noise does not increase with time. At 2 fs, float32 possibly drifts by up to approximately 1 meV/atom per ns. A thermostat removes a drift of this size.

    Large boxes in LAMMPS

    A float32 LAMMPS bundle receives float32 atom positions. The round-off of the bond vectors thus increases with the box size. Measure the error in a box of the size that you use. The ASE calculator calculates bond vectors in float64, so this does not apply to it. With the kernel, mixed precision keeps float64 positions.

    Mixed precision for the kernels¶

    The C++ kernel and the CUDA kernel for linear ACE have a mixed-precision mode. It is off by default. In this mode, the kernel calculates the product basis in float32. It calculates the bond vectors, the radial basis, the spherical harmonics and the sums over neighbours in float64. Positions, energies and forces stay float64.

    Mixed precision is faster only for large models, where the product basis uses most of the time:

    model (silicon, linear ACE) CPU, LAMMPS, 128 cores A100, ASE calculator
    o4d20 (5,476 basis functions) 1.4 times 1.4 times
    o4d16 1.1 times 1.1 to 1.2 times
    o4d14 1.1 times no gain
    o4d12 and order 3 no gain no gain

    The errors are those of float32 for the product basis. For the silicon model o4d20, the maximum force error is approximately 0.05 meV/Å, and the energy has noise of approximately 0.1 meV in a cell of 200 atoms. For o4d16, the maximum force error is approximately 0.2 meV/Å and the noise is 1 meV. The errors are larger for smaller models, as they are in float32. The smallest models also have an energy offset that is the same for each atom: approximately 0.6 meV for o3d8 and 0.2 meV for o3d10 in bulk silicon. With a force-only optimiser, mixed precision changed no property of o4d20 by more than 0.013 of its tolerance in the silicon benchmark.

    Energy line searches fail in mixed precision

    The energy noise is the same type as in float32. Optimisers with an energy line search (L-BFGS or CG with Armijo or Wolfe tests) can fail near a minimum. Use an optimiser that uses only forces, for example ASE ODE12r or FIRE, or min_style fire in LAMMPS. The table in Tasks that can use float32 applies to mixed precision also.

    Use mixed precision in place of float32 in these conditions:

    • On a CPU. The C++ kernel has no float32 mode.
    • In LAMMPS on a GPU with a large box. A float32 bundle receives float32 positions, and its force error increases with the distance from the origin (0.05 meV/Å at the origin, 0.8 meV/Å at 1000 Å for o4d20). A mixed-precision bundle receives float64 positions, and its error does not change.

    On a GPU, a float32 calculation is faster than mixed precision and has approximately the same force error in a small box.

    To set mixed precision:

    • ASE: ACECalculator("model.npz", ffi=True, ffi_precision="mixed"). calc.ffi["precision"] then gives "mixed".
    • Batches: BatchCalculator(..., ffi=True, ffi_precision="mixed").
    • LAMMPS: aj export --ffi --ffi-precision mixed, or export_lammps(..., ffi=True, ffi_precision="mixed"). The bundle records it as ace_jax.ffi_precision.

    Mixed precision needs the kernel and float64. It uses the same compiled kernel library as float64.

    Check a new task¶

    1. Measure the float32 error of the model on structures of the task.
    2. Compare the force error with the force tolerance of the task. Examples are fmax, the NEB tolerance and the thermal forces.
    3. Multiply the energy error per atom by the number of atoms. Compare the result with the energy differences that you need.
    4. Do a small number of calculations again in float64. For statistical results, for example free energies, also do a short float64 run.
    5. Use float32 only if it is faster on your hardware.

    Set the precision¶

    • ASE: ACECalculator("model.npz", dtype=np.float32). See ASE calculators.
    • LAMMPS: export_lammps(..., dtype="float32") or aj export --dtype float32. See LAMMPS export.
    • Mixed precision of the kernels: ffi_precision="mixed". See Mixed precision for the kernels.
