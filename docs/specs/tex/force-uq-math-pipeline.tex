\paragraph{Summary.} The served force uncertainty is still a shape times a per-group scale.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The \emph{shape} is now the per-atom $3\times3$ block $V(x)$ of an exact, centred,
  delete-one-cluster jackknife covariance, with clusters given by spatial blocks within large cells.
  Its trace $v(x)=\operatorname{tr}V(x)$ is the isotropic shape.
  \item The \emph{scale} is split in two. \code{forces\_std} uses a per-group rms factor
  $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}$, which does not depend on $\alpha$. \code{forces\_q} uses a per-group
  conformal quantile $q_g$, computed with each configuration weighted equally.
  \item Calibration scores now take numerator and denominator from the same hold-out posterior
  $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$.
\end{itemize}
Within a group, the ordering of atoms by $\sigma$ is the ordering given by the shape. Across groups
it changes only through ratios of the group scales.

\section{Model and data (unchanged)}

Each atom $i$ with environment $x_i$ has a site descriptor $\phi(x_i)\in\R^L$: the ACE product basis
in species-major blocks, plus pair blocks. The model is linear in the coefficients $c$:
\begin{equation}
E(R) = \sum_i \phi(x_i)\cdot c + \sum_i E^0_{Z_i}.
\end{equation}
A training configuration contributes energy, force and virial observations, each linear in $c$:
\begin{equation}
\phi_E = \sum_i \phi(x_i),\qquad
\phi_{F,i\alpha} = -\frac{\partial \phi_E}{\partial r_{i\alpha}},\qquad
\phi_{V,\alpha\beta} = -\sum_i r_{i\alpha}\frac{\partial\phi_E}{\partial r_{i\beta}}.
\end{equation}
Each row has a structural weight $w$ (per-atom energy normalisation and per-type weights) and a
noise scale $\sigma_q$ for its quantity $q\in\{E,F,V\}$. The whitened rows and targets are
$\psi=\phi\,w/\sigma_q$ and $\tilde y = y\,w/\sigma_q$, where $y$ is the label minus the $E^0$
baseline.

\section{Prior and posterior (unchanged)}

The prior is $c\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Lambda^{-1})$ with $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}\big(\Gamma_j^2 e^{a_{k(j)}}\big)$.
Here $\Gamma_j$ is the smoothness prior of column $j$, and $a_k$ is the ARD log-precision of body
order $k(j)\in\{2,3,4\}$; pair columns and correlation-order-1 columns both count as 2-body. The
posterior is Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
A=\sum_{\text{rows}}\psi\psi^\T+\Lambda,\qquad \cbar = A^{-1}\sum_{\text{rows}}\psi\tilde y,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(c)=A^{-1}.
\end{equation}
We work in the prior-scaled system $S=D^{-1}AD^{-1}=LL^\T$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}\Gamma$, and
floor $a_k\ge a_{\mathrm{floor}}$ so that $\operatorname{cond}(S)\le10^{14}$.

\section{Hyperparameters: type-II maximum likelihood (unchanged)}

The hyperparameters are $h=(\log\sigma_E,\log\sigma_F,\log\sigma_V,a_2,a_3,a_4)$. The evidence uses
the per-quantity statistics $G_q=\sum\psi\psi^\T$, $b_q$, $y^\T y_q$ and $n_q$, and up to constants is
\begin{equation}
\log p(\mathcal D\mid h) = -\tfrac12\sum_q\frac{y^\T y_q}{\sigma_q^2}
+\tfrac12 b^\T A^{-1}b-\tfrac12\log|A|+\tfrac12\log|\Lambda|-\sum_q n_q\log\sigma_q .
\end{equation}
The \emph{joint} mode maximises it over all six components of $h$, with L-BFGS-B on the
gradient-normalised objective. The \emph{sequential} mode fixes $\sigma_q$ at the linear MAP and
fits only the $a_k$.

\section{The hold-out protocol (revised)}\label{sec:holdout}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stratified split by configuration.} Compute the Mondrian group of every training atom
(Section~\ref{sec:groups}), with the band edges computed from all of $T$. Assign each configuration $c$ the stratum
$\varsigma(c)$, the \emph{most extreme} group it populates: the highest distortion band, with ties
broken in favour of $z\ne z^\star$. Within each stratum, randomly assign a fraction $f=0.2$ of
configurations to $\Tval$ and the rest to $\Tfit$, rounding so that every stratum with at least two
configurations has at least one on each side. Strata with fewer than two configurations are merged
into the next less extreme stratum. A crack cell is therefore split as a crack cell, even though
most of its atoms are bulk-like.


\item \textbf{Hold-out posterior.} Fit $h$ on $\Tfit$, giving $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ with mean
$\cfit$, factor $L_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and the shape factor $\Qt_{\mathrm{fit}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:shape},
built over the clusters of $\Tfit$ only). For each held-out atom $i\in\Tval$, record the error
$e_i=F_i-\hat F_i(\cfit)$ and the hold-out shape $V_{\mathrm{fit}}(x_i)$. Both are out-of-sample,
because no row of $c(i)$ is in $\Tfit$.

\item \textbf{Served posterior.} Refit on all of $T$, starting from $h_{\mathrm{fit}}$. This gives
$P$ with mean $\cbar$, factor $L$ and shape factor $\Qt$.
\end{enumerate}

$P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is needed only to produce the scores; $\Qt_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is not stored.

\section{Sandwich clusters}\label{sec:clusters}

The sandwich treats clusters of rows as independent units. The clusters $\mathcal{K}$ partition
the rows of $T$ as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Small configurations} are a single cluster containing all their energy, force and virial
rows. A configuration counts as small unless it can hold at least two blocks of side $\ell$ along some
lattice direction.

\item \textbf{Large configurations} are split into:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item one cluster per spatial block, containing the force rows of the atoms in that block; and
  \item one cluster containing the energy row and the six virial rows, since these rows couple every
  atom in the cell.
  \end{itemize}
Blocks are cells of a grid with $n_a=\max\big(1,\lfloor w_a/\ell\rfloor\big)$ divisions along lattice
direction $a$. Here $w_a$ is the cell width perpendicular to the other two lattice vectors, or the
extent of the atomic bounding box along a non-periodic direction. Atoms are assigned by their
fractional coordinates, respecting periodicity.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Block size.} A force depends on environments within $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$ of neighbours within
$r_{\mathrm{cut}}$, so the minimum sensible size is $\ell=2r_{\mathrm{cut}}$. The default is
$\ell=3r_{\mathrm{cut}}$. $\ell$ is validated by the plateau check of Section~\ref{sec:validation}:
increase $\ell$ until $v(x)$ and the $\lambda_g$ stop changing. This is standard practice for
cluster-robust variances \cite{cameron2015}. Sub-clustering acts only on the sandwich, which is built
from $T$: with $\ell=3r_{\mathrm{cut}}=18.75$\,\AA\ every bench365 training configuration is small, and
large cells that appear only in a calibration set $U$ never enter the sandwich. The feature matters
once large cells are in training, which is the fracture use case, and is validated in that setting.



\paragraph{Two kinds of unit.} Sandwich clusters are an ingredient of the shape only. The hold-out
split, the conformal quantiles and the bootstrap all operate on whole configurations
(Sections~\ref{sec:holdout} and~\ref{sec:scale}).

\section{The shape: exact centred jackknife covariance (revised)}\label{sec:shape}

\subsection{Leverage-corrected cluster scores}
For cluster $k$ with stacked whitened rows $\Psi_k\in\R^{n_k\times L}$ and residuals at the served mean
$\rho_k=\tilde y_k-\Psi_k\cbar$, define the cluster hat block and the leverage-corrected (PRESS) score
\begin{equation}
H_{kk}=\Psi_kA^{-1}\Psi_k^\T = W_k^\T W_k,\quad W_k=L^{-1}D^{-1}\Psi_k^\T,\qquad
\gt_k=\Psi_k^\T(I-H_{kk})^{-1}\rho_k .
\label{eq:press}
\end{equation}
With $h$ held fixed, deleting the rows of cluster $k$ changes the posterior mean by exactly
\begin{equation}
\cbar-\cbar_{(-k)} = A^{-1}\gt_k,
\label{eq:dfbeta}
\end{equation}
the cluster DFBETA \cite{cook1982}. Here $(I-H_{kk})^{-1}\rho_k$ are the residuals that cluster $k$
would have if it were left out of the fit.

\paragraph{Well-posedness.} Since $\Lambda\succ0$, we have $A\succ\Psi_k^\T\Psi_k$, so every eigenvalue
of $H_{kk}$ lies in $[0,1)$. Hence $I-H_{kk}$ is always invertible. Unlike unregularised OLS, no single
cluster can saturate the fit.

\paragraph{Computation.} If $n_k\le L$, form $W_k$ and solve the $n_k\times n_k$ system in
\eqref{eq:press}. If $n_k>L$, use the push-through identity
\begin{equation}
A^{-1}\gt_k = A_{(-k)}^{-1}\Psi_k^\T\rho_k,\qquad A_{(-k)}=A-\Psi_k^\T\Psi_k,
\end{equation}
with a rank-$n_k$ Cholesky downdate of $S$. Clusters are independent and can be batched (\code{vmap}
over padded $n_k$ buckets). Sub-clustering keeps most $n_k$ to $3N_k\lesssim10^3$.

\paragraph{Fallback for very large clusters.} Optionally, apply the correction per row block
instead: $(1-h)^{-1}$ for the energy row, the $3\times3$ force block of each atom, and the $6\times6$
virial block. This is HC3-like rather than an exact deletion, and it ignores coupling between atoms
within a cluster. It is never the default.

\prev{the per-row leverages of the bench365 training rows have median $0.020$ and 99th percentile
$0.087$, so the correction $(I-H_{kk})^{-1}$ is a few per cent for most clusters. Its effect is
concentrated in the high-leverage ones, which validation item~2 reports.}

\subsection{Centred meat and the shape}
Let $\bar g=|\mathcal{K}|^{-1}\sum_k\gt_k$. The centred meat is
\begin{equation}
\Mt=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}(\gt_k-\bar g)(\gt_k-\bar g)^\T .
\end{equation}
Centring removes the mean deletion effect. Without it, the meat contains a term from the prior's
gradient: for the uncorrected scores, $\sum_k\Psi_k^\T\rho_k=\Lambda\cbar$ at the MAP. With centring,
$A^{-1}\Mt A^{-1}$ is the delete-one-cluster jackknife covariance of $\cbar$, up to the factor
$(|\mathcal{K}|-1)/|\mathcal{K}|$, which the scale absorbs. It is the CR3 cluster-robust estimator
\cite{mackinnon2023}, and the exact counterpart of the infinitesimal jackknife used previously. As
before it is a Huber--White sandwich \cite{huber1967,muller2013}, and $\kappa^2A^{-1}$ is the
homoscedastic special case $\Mt\propto A$.

Define
\begin{equation}
\Qt=S^{-1}D^{-1}\big[\gt_1-\bar g\;\cdots\;\gt_{|\mathcal{K}|}-\bar g\big]\in\R^{L\times|\mathcal{K}|},
\qquad u_\alpha(x)=D^{-1}\phi_\alpha(x)^\T ,
\end{equation}
where $\phi_\alpha(x)$ are the raw, unweighted force rows of a target atom. The served shape is
\begin{equation}
V(x)_{\alpha\beta}=u_\alpha^\T\Qt\Qt^\T u_\beta,\qquad v(x)=\operatorname{tr}V(x)=\sum_\alpha\big\|\Qt^\T u_\alpha\big\|^2 .
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
$v(x)$ replaces $m^2(x)$. $V(x)$ is the full anisotropic block, and comes at no extra cost because the
projections $\Qt^\T u_\alpha$ are already computed for $v$. Since
$\phi_\alpha A^{-1}(\cbar-\cbar_{(-k)})$ is the change in the prediction at $x$ when cluster $k$ is
deleted, $v(x)$ is the spread of the prediction over cluster deletions. It is large where the
prediction leans on clusters the model fits badly.

\prev{the uncentred, uncorrected configuration-clustered sandwich $m^2$ ranked within-family errors
with Spearman $\rho=0.30$--$0.37$, against $0.19$--$0.26$ for the epistemic $s^2$. A likelihood fit of
$a\,s^2+b\,m^2$ gave $a=0$.}

\paragraph{Epistemic variant (\code{-{}-ard-variance kappa}).} This is unchanged:
$V_\kappa(x)_{\alpha\beta}=\phi_\alpha A^{-1}\phi_\beta^\T$, with $v_\kappa=\operatorname{tr}V_\kappa$.
Everything below applies to either variant.

\subsection{Storage}
The cluster count $|\mathcal{K}|$ can exceed $L$ once large cells are sub-clustered. Since only
$\Qt\Qt^\T$ enters \eqref{eq:shape}, we store a factor $R$ with $RR^\T=\Qt\Qt^\T$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $R=\Qt$ if $|\mathcal{K}|\le L$;
\item otherwise $R=U_r\Sigma_r$, from the thin SVD $\Qt=U\Sigma V^\T$ (so that $\Qt\Qt^\T=U\Sigma^2U^\T$),
keeping the leading $r$ singular triplets.
\end{itemize}
An optional truncation keeps the smallest $r$ with $\sum_{j\le r}\sigma_j^2\ge\tau\sum_j\sigma_j^2$
(default $\tau=1$, i.e.\ exact). Storage is $L\times\min(|\mathcal{K}|,L)$.

\section{Calibration scores (revised)}\label{sec:scores}

For $i\in\Tval$, both the error and the shape come from $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:holdout}):
\begin{align}
\text{isotropic:}\quad & s_i=\frac{|e_i|}{\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{fit}}(x_i)/3}}, \label{eq:siso}\\
\text{anisotropic:}\quad & s_i=\sqrt{e_i^\T\big(V_{\mathrm{fit}}(x_i)+\epsilon_i I\big)^{-1}e_i},
\qquad \epsilon_i=\epsilon\,\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}V_{\mathrm{fit}}(x_i). \label{eq:saniso}
\end{align}
The default regulariser is $\epsilon=10^{-3}$. It matters only where $V_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is close to
rank-deficient. Under the working model
$e\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\lambda^2\,\tfrac{v}{3}I_3)$ (isotropic) or $e\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\lambda^2V)$
(anisotropic), $s/\lambda\sim\chi_3$. The conformal step does not rely on this model; only the
reading of \code{forces\_std} does. The mode is chosen at fit time (\code{-{}-force-shape iso|aniso};
default \code{aniso}: on the bench365 v3 validation the anisotropic region is the only uncalibrated variant meeting the targets, Section~\ref{sec:validation}), and every scale in
Section~\ref{sec:scale} is computed in that mode.

\paragraph{Transfer assumption.} The scores come from a model trained on about $(1-f)|T|$
configurations; the scale is applied to the shape of the model trained on $T$. The shape is a
parameter variance, scaling roughly as $1/N$, whereas the error is dominated by approximation
error ($\lambda^2\approx39$ on bench365). The ratio of squared error to shape is therefore
unchanged only if the error is variance-dominated; if it is bias-dominated, $\lambda^2$ grows
roughly in proportion to $N$, and the hold-out scale underestimates the served one by up to
$(1-f)^{-1/2}$. Writing $\lambda\propto N^{\beta}$, the variance-dominated limit is $\beta=0$ and
the bias-dominated one $\beta=\tfrac12$.

\paragraph{Transfer exponent.} The served scale replaces the uncorrected hold-out scale by an
extrapolation in $N$ estimated in every fit. $T_{\mathrm{fit}}$ is split again by the same
stratified rule into $T_{\mathrm{fit2}}$, with $N_{\mathrm{fit2}}\approx(1-f)N_{\mathrm{fit}}$
configurations. A second hold-out posterior $P_{\mathrm{fit2}}$ is fitted on it, with its own
jackknife shape, and scores the same $T_{\mathrm{val}}$ atoms. Let $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ be
the configuration-weighted rms scales of the $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{fit2}}$ scores,
pooled over all groups with the weights of Section~\ref{sec:scale}, over the $T_{\mathrm{val}}$ atoms
at which both shapes are positive. Then
\[
  \hat\beta=\frac{\log(\lambda_1/\lambda_2)}{\log(N_{\mathrm{fit}}/N_{\mathrm{fit2}})},\qquad
  \beta=\min\bigl(\max(\hat\beta,0),\tfrac12\bigr),\qquad
  t=(N/N_{\mathrm{fit}})^{\beta},
\]
and every calibration score is multiplied by $t$ before the per-group scales are formed. The clip
keeps $\beta$ between the two limits above, and a clip is reported; if $\hat\beta$ is undefined
($N_{\mathrm{fit2}}=N_{\mathrm{fit}}$, or a non-finite scale) $\beta=\tfrac12$, the conservative
limit. One pooled $\beta$ serves all groups. $\beta$ is a single-split estimate; because the
extrapolation step equals the baseline ($N/N_{\mathrm{fit}}\approx N_{\mathrm{fit}}/N_{\mathrm{fit2}}$),
the factor's relative error is about that of $\lambda_1/\lambda_2$, and the clip bounds the factor
to at most $(1-f)^{-1/2}$. On bench365 the $f$ sweep gives $\log\lambda\propto0.37\log N_{\mathrm{fit}}$, and
$(3680/2944)^{0.37}=1.086$ matches the served rms-$z$ deficit of $1.087$. The cost is one more evidence fit and jackknife
shape on about $(1-f)^2|T|$ configurations. It is still checked by repeating the protocol at
$f\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$.

\prev{a test-set refit of the scale changed it by $\times0.968$, i.e.\ the served $\sigma$ was very
slightly conservative, not the up-to-$\times1.12$ underestimate the bias-dominated limit allows at
$f=0.2$. (The own-cluster correction, $\lambda=6.27$ against $6.11$, moved it by $2.7\%$.)}

\section{The scale (revised)}\label{sec:scale}

\subsection{Units and weights}
For group $g$, let $\mathcal{C}_g$ be the set of calibration configurations with at least one atom in
$g$, $\ncfg=|\mathcal{C}_g|$, and $n_{c,g}$ the number of atoms of $c$ in $g$. Each calibration atom
$i\in c\cap g$ carries the weight $1/n_{c,g}$, so every configuration has total weight 1 in every group
it populates. A crack cell is one unit in the tip group, whether it has 3 tip atoms or 30.

\subsection{\code{forces\_std}: per-group rms scale}
\begin{gather}
(\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}})^2=\frac{1}{3\,\ncfg}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_g}\frac{1}{n_{c,g}}\sum_{i\in c\cap g}s_i^2,\\
\code{forces\_std}(x)=\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_{g(x)}\sqrt{v(x)},\qquad
\code{forces\_cov}(x)=\big(\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_{g(x)}\big)^2 V(x).
\end{gather}
This is the configuration-weighted Gaussian NLL optimum within each group. It does not depend on
$\alpha$ and makes no coverage claim; it is the scale to use when propagating uncertainty or when a
Gaussian is wanted. \code{forces\_cov} is served in both modes. In isotropic mode it uses the isotropic
$\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}$, so its trace, $\code{forces\_std}^2$, is calibrated but its orientation is
the uncalibrated shape of $V(x)$.

\subsection{\code{forces\_q}: per-group conformal quantile}
The configuration-weighted (pooled-CDF) score distribution of group $g$, with a test point at
$+\infty$, is
\begin{equation}
\hat F_g(t)=\frac{1}{\ncfg+1}\Bigg[\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_g}\frac{1}{n_{c,g}}\sum_{i\in c\cap g}
\mathbb{1}\{s_i\le t\}\;+\;\mathbb{1}\{+\infty\le t\}\Bigg],
\qquad q_g=\inf\{t:\hat F_g(t)\ge1-\alpha\}.
\label{eq:pooled}
\end{equation}
When no neighbouring group qualifies, the fallback pool is all atoms with the same weights $1/n_{c,g}$
(a configuration spanning $k$ groups has total weight $k$), and the $+\infty$ test point carries the weight
$W/\ncfg$ of one configuration's mean total weight, $W=\sum_i w_i$:
$\hat F(t)=\big[\sum_i w_i\mathbb{1}\{s_i\le t\}+(W/\ncfg)\mathbb{1}\{+\infty\le t\}\big]/\big(W(1+1/\ncfg)\big)$,
which reduces to \eqref{eq:pooled} for a single group ($W=\ncfg$); in every pool $q$ is finite iff
$\ncfg\ge\lceil(1-\alpha)/\alpha\rceil$.
The served regions are
\begin{align}
\text{isotropic:}\quad & \{\Delta F: |\Delta F|\le \code{forces\_q}(x)\},\qquad
\code{forces\_q}(x)=q_{g(x)}\sqrt{v(x)/3},\\
\text{anisotropic:}\quad & \big\{\Delta F:\Delta F^\T\big(V(x)+\epsilon(x)I\big)^{-1}\Delta F\le q_{g(x)}^2\big\},
\end{align}
where $\epsilon(x)$ is defined as in \eqref{eq:saniso}. In anisotropic mode, $q_{g(x)}$ is served
per atom (\code{forces\_q\_mahal}) together with $V(x)$. The scalar \code{forces\_q} is then the largest
semi-axis, $q_{g(x)}\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(V+\epsilon I)}$, for convenience.

\paragraph{Coverage statement.} Assume calibration and target configurations are exchangeable, and
take the target to be a uniformly random group-$g$ atom of a new configuration. Then the region has
coverage $\approx1-\alpha$, with an approximation error controlled by $\ncfg$, not by the atom count.
\eqref{eq:pooled} is the pooled-CDF construction of \cite{dunn2023}. Their exact finite-sample
constructions (double conformal, subsampling) are the fallback if a formal bound is needed; they
are not implemented in this PR. No Gaussianity is assumed. Because $\hat F_g$ counts configurations,
the finite-sample correction is at the level of $\ncfg$.

\paragraph{Merging.} Groups with $\ncfg<\nmin$ (default 20) are merged with their neighbouring
distortion band within the same $[z=z^\star]$ flag, and then across the flag. The merges are reported.

\paragraph{Reported per group, and per atom.} Each atom is served its group id, \code{forces\_group}.
The per-group table stored with the model holds $\ncfg$ (split into configurations from $\Tval$ and
from $U$, Section~\ref{sec:calsets}), total atoms, $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}$, $q_g$, the merges, and the
Gaussianity diagnostic
\begin{equation}
r_g=\frac{q_g}{\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}\,\chiinv}.
\end{equation}
$r_g\approx1$ means that reading \code{forces\_std} as a Gaussian reproduces the conformal coverage
in that group. $r_g>1$ means the score distribution has heavier tails than $\chi_3$; within the group,
\code{forces\_q} is $r_g$ times the radius implied by \code{forces\_std} read as a Gaussian. $r_g$ is
constant within a group, so it is not served per atom: \code{forces\_group} joins each atom to it,
and to $\ncfg$, the pool composition and the merges.

\prev{in distribution, with one group, the conformal 90\% quantile of the isotropic score was
$q=2.48$, against $\lambda\,\chi_3^{-1}(0.9)=2.50$ for the rms scale, i.e.\ $r\approx0.99$.}

\paragraph{What is invariant.} The mean $\cbar$; the energy and virial predictions; the shape; and the
ordering by $\sigma$ within a group. Across groups, the relative $\sigma$ changes by
$\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_g/\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_{g'}$ and the relative radius by $q_g/q_{g'}$.

\section{The group feature (Mondrian; band edges revised)}\label{sec:groups}

Three sets of constants are fixed at fit time and stored with the model: $r_1$, the first minimum of
the training radial distribution function; $z^\star$, the modal training coordination; and the band
edges. For each atom,
\begin{equation}
z_i=\#\{j:r_{ij}<r_1\},\qquad
d_i=\frac{\operatorname{std}\{r_{ij}:r_{ij}<r_1\}}{\operatorname{mean}\{r_{ij}:r_{ij}<r_1\}}
\quad(\text{NaN if }z_i<2).
\end{equation}
The band edges are the p50, p90 and p99 quantiles of $d$ over all atoms of $T$. The same edges
stratify the hold-out split (Section~\ref{sec:holdout}) and are stored with the model. They depend
only on geometry, never on labels or scores, so letting $\Tval$'s geometry into three quantiles over
thousands of atoms has a negligible effect on exchangeability, and the p99 edge is estimated from
25\% more atoms. What matters is that the edges are frozen at fit time: \code{calibrate} never changes
which group an atom is in. The group is
$g=\operatorname{band}(d)\times[z=z^\star]$, so $G=8$ before merging.

\prev{on the big cells, miscoverage tracked distortion ($\sigma$ grew $\times1.7$ from low to high
distortion while the error grew $\times2.0$) and coordination (bulk-like calibrated, surface-like
conservative, strained-shell overconfident). In distribution, coverage was flat across these groups.}

%======================================================================
\section{Calibration sets (revised)}\label{sec:calsets}

\paragraph{Default (inside the fit).} The pool is $\Tval$, with the scores of Section~\ref{sec:scores}.

\paragraph{\code{aj calibrate} (target regime).} A set $U$ of labelled configurations, disjoint from $T$.
Errors are those of the served model, $e_u=F_u-\hat F_u(\cbar)$. The shape is the served $V(x_u)$,
which is out-of-sample because $U\cap T=\emptyset$. Scores follow \eqref{eq:siso} or
\eqref{eq:saniso} with $V$ in place of $V_{\mathrm{fit}}$. Then, for each group $g$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item if $U$ has at least $\nmin$ configurations in $g$, the pool for $g$ is $U$ alone (\emph{per-group
replace}, the default);
\item otherwise the pool is $\Tval\cup U$, with configuration weights as in Section~\ref{sec:scale}.
\end{itemize}
\code{-{}-append} forces $\Tval\cup U$ in every group, and \code{-{}-replace} forces $U$ alone in every
group (merging as needed). $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}$ and $q_g$ are recomputed. Each group reports its
pool composition: the number of configurations from $\Tval$ and from $U$. With equal configuration
weights these counts give the pool's exact makeup. Mixed groups are where the transfer assumption
(Section~\ref{sec:scores}) matters most, since $\Tval$ scores come from $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ and $U$ scores
from the served model.

\paragraph{Rationale.} A target atom resembling $U$ should be calibrated against $U$. Mixing in
$\Tval$ scores from easier atoms of the same group dilutes the quantile and gives the coverage of a
mixture.



\prev{leaving out one realisation at a time over 10 crack realisations (30 cells), with $\Tval\cup U$
pooled: whole crack cell $0.899$, crack tip $0.885$ (fold range $0.864$--$0.900$), dislocations
$0.92$--$0.93$.}

\section{The support diagnostic (revised features; not served)}\label{sec:support}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Features.} For each species, fit a whitened PCA of the site descriptor $\phi(x)$ (that
species' block) on the training atoms. Keep components up to 99\% explained variance, capped at 64.
The site descriptor is rotation-invariant and includes all body orders. The force-row projections
$\Qt^\T u_\alpha$ are not used as features, because they rotate with the structure.

\item \textbf{Density ratio.} For each species, fit an L2 logistic classifier (target $=1$,
calibration $=0$, class-balanced) on these features. Choose the L2 strength by 5-fold cross-validation
grouped by configuration. This gives $w(x)\propto P(t\mid x)/P(\mathrm{cal}\mid x)$.

\item \textbf{Weighted quantile} \cite{tibshirani2019}. Calibration atom $i$ has mass $w(x_i)/n_{c(i)}$,
which combines the shift weight with the configuration weight. The test atom has mass $w(x)$. With
$W$ the total mass,
\begin{equation}
q_w(x)=Q_{1-\alpha}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathrm{cal}}\frac{w(x_i)}{n_{c(i)}W}\delta_{s_i}+\frac{w(x)}{W}\delta_{+\infty}\Big).
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{Report} \code{support\_q} $=q_w(x)$ and \code{support\_ok} $=[q_w<\infty]$ per atom,
and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\sum_i p_i)^2/\sum_i p_i^2$ per species, computed over the calibration masses
$p_i=w(x_i)/n_{c(i)}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Caveats.} Richer features give more extreme ratios and so a smaller $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$;
this is the honest outcome. The diagnostic assumes that the score distribution given the classifier
features is unchanged from calibration to target. The labels are deterministic in the full structure,
but not in the local environment (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), so this is an approximation even with full
features. As before, the diagnostic is not used to scale $\sigma$.

\prev{with whitened 2-body features and no target labels, the check flagged $10.7\%$ of crack-tip
atoms, against $0.4$--$0.5\%$ for the dislocations; the per-species $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ was 10--864 out of
about 12.5k.}

\section{End-to-end summary}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item[fit] Data $\to$ group features (fixing $r_1$, $z^\star$ and band edges from $T$) $\to$
stratified split $\to$ type-II ML on $\Tfit$ $\to$ $P_{\mathrm{fit}}$ $\to$ clusters, PRESS scores,
centred $\Qt_{\mathrm{fit}}$ $\to$ $V_{\mathrm{fit}}(x_i)$ and errors $e_i$ on $\Tval$ $\to$ scores
$s_i$ $\to$ refit on $T$ $\to$ $P$, clusters, PRESS scores, centred $\Qt$ $\to$ stored factor $R$ $\to$
per-group $\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_g$ and $q_g$ (configuration-weighted, merged below $\nmin$).
\item[calibrate] Labelled target set $U$ $\to$ scores with the served model and shape $\to$ per-group
replace (or append) $\to$ recompute $\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}_g$ and $q_g$.
\item[serve] Shape $V(x)$ from the projections $R^\T u_\alpha(x)$ $\to$\\ \code{forces\_std},
\code{forces\_cov}, \code{forces\_q} (and \code{forces\_q\_mahal} in anisotropic mode) and
\code{forces\_group}; the per-group table is stored with the model.
\item[diagnose] An unlabelled target $\to$ descriptor PCA $\to$ $w(x)$ $\to$ weighted quantile $\to$
\code{support\_ok}, \code{support\_q}, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
\end{description}

\section{Validation for this PR}\label{sec:validation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact deletion (unit test).} On a small system, refit without cluster $k$, with $h$ fixed,
and check \eqref{eq:dfbeta} to machine precision. Do this for a whole-configuration cluster, a spatial
block, and an energy/virial cluster.
\item \textbf{Leverage report.} Report $\lambda_{\max}(H_{kk})$ per cluster, and whether the high-leverage
clusters are the crack-like ones.
\item \textbf{Block size, with crack cells in training.} In each leave-one-realisation fold, put the
other nine realisations' crack cells into $T$, refit, and sweep $\ell/r_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\{2,3,4,6,\infty\}$
($\infty$ = whole configurations), recording $v(x)$ and the $\lambda_g$. The default is the smallest
$\ell$ on the plateau. The same run compares tip coverage with tip-like data in \emph{training} (which
changes the shape) against tip-like data only in \emph{calibration} (which changes only the scale);
both are reported.
\item \textbf{Transfer.} Compute the $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{rms}}$ and $q_g$ at $f\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$.
\item \textbf{Ablation on bench365} (10-realisation leave-one-out as before), for each of:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item the 30 September baseline;
  \item $+$A;
  \item $+$A$+$B (whole-configuration clusters);
  \item $+$A$+$B$+$blocks;
  \item $+$anisotropic.
  \end{itemize}
Measure:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Spearman $\rho(\sigma,|e|)$;
  \item the global $\lambda^{\mathrm{rms}}$ for each variant;
  \item per-group coverage, configuration- and atom-weighted, with configuration-bootstrap 90\%
  intervals ($B=1000$);
  \item crack-tip coverage;
  \item mean region volume at equal coverage.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Assumptions and known limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exchangeability at configuration level, within a group.} The coverage statement is
approximate, with error controlled by $\ncfg$. $\ncfg$ and the merges are reported.
\item \textbf{Transfer} from the hold-out model to the served model (Section~\ref{sec:scores}). This is an
empirical assumption. The per-fit exponent extrapolates the scale from two hold-out sizes to $|T|$,
assuming a power law in $N$ with exponent in $[0,\tfrac12]$. It is checked across $f$.
\item \textbf{The shape is a parameter variance used as a proxy for where approximation error is large.}
$v(x)$ measures how much the prediction at $x$ depends on which clusters were in the fit, not the
approximation error itself. The Spearman $\rho=0.30$--$0.37$ measures only how well the one ranks the
other.
\item \textbf{Independence of sandwich clusters.} Spatial blocks assume that errors are uncorrelated
across block boundaries beyond about $2r_{\mathrm{cut}}$. The block-size plateau tests this.
\item \textbf{Hyperparameters fixed in the jackknife.} The deletion jackknife holds $h$ fixed, so the
shape carries no uncertainty from the hyperparameters.
\item \textbf{Groups chosen from benchmark evidence.} A shift the groups do not resolve (chemical
short-range order, a new phase) is covered only marginally. The support diagnostic is the guard.
\item \textbf{The residual crack-tip gap} (about 1.5 points in the previous version) is to be
re-measured after A, B and sub-clustering.
\item \textbf{Forces only.} The energy and virial variances remain the unscaled posterior variances.
\item \textbf{Locality acts as effective noise.} The labels carry no noise, but the descriptor
truncates the environment at $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$. Conditional on $x$, the label is therefore not
deterministic: non-local elastic and electronic contributions act as aleatoric noise relative to the
model. The scale absorbs its size; nothing models where it is larger. This is a candidate explanation
for any gap at the tip that survives the revisions. It can be tested by comparing tip errors from a fit
with a larger $r_{\mathrm{cut}}$ (out of scope for this PR).
\end{enumerate}
