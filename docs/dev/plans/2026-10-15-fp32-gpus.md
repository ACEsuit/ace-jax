# fp32 GPUs: plan

**Goal:** Use fp32-strong, fp64-poor GPUs effectively for both inference and
fitting. Examples: moriarty's RTX A4500 (fp64 at 1/64 of the fp32 rate), Avon's
RTX 6000 and Blythe's L40, plus the HPC trend towards less fp64 hardware.

**Thesis:** Rounding the *data* to fp32 is harmless. Each entry of Φ changes by
about 1e-7 relative, which is far below DFT label noise. What breaks a fit is an
*algorithm* that squares the condition number (a Gram matrix, the normal
equations) or a factorisation run in fp32. κ(Φ) reaches about 1e8 on
near-interpolating fits, and that is above 1/ε₃₂ ≈ 1.7e7. So:

- The N×L work (basis evaluation, design rows, forces by autodiff) is almost
  all of the flops. It moves to fp32.
- The L×L work stays fp64: QR merges, the LML, the posterior and the θ
  optimisation. It is small enough for the CPU, or for emulated fp64 later.
- Reductions that cancel (energy sums, virials, the readout) accumulate in
  fp64 or with compensation.

The streamed sufficient-statistics design (rows → `QRStats`) already
separates the large fp32-tolerant data from the small precision-critical
state. `stats.host_qr_stream` already runs the merges on the host.

## Step 1: measure (this branch)

Nothing changes in `src/` yet. Scripts live in `bench/fp32/`, and results go in
`docs/dev/fp32-results.md`.

1. **Row error, fp32 vs fp64.**
   - Inputs: the GAP-18 Si accuracy-cost training set (subsampled) and the
     `ace_per_quantity_noise` fitted bases o3d8, o3d12, o4d12 and o4d16
     (L = 62 to 1461).
   - Compute the raw E/F/V rows in a float32 process (x64 off) on the GPU, and
     in float64.
   - Report the error per row type, relative to column norms. Also run fp32
     with JAX's default matmul precision (TF32 on Ampere) to size that trap.
2. **Conditioning.** Weight the rows as the fit does (`solve._weighted_rows`,
   the MAP σ from `theta_map.json`), and augment them with the prior block
   diag(γ/σ_c). Report κ for:
   - the raw weighted design;
   - the design with Jacobi column scaling;
   - the prior-augmented design (what the QR actually factors).
3. **Does fp32 data change the fit?**
   - Solve the prior-augmented least squares in fp64 on Φ₆₄ and on Φ₃₂
     (cast to fp64).
   - Compare the coefficients, and the E/F/V predictions against the training
     RMSE.
   - Also solve fully in fp32 (fp32 QR) to show where that fails.
4. **Readout cancellation and fp32 inference.**
   - For the fitted models, compute the per-site ratio Σ|c_k B_k| / |Σ c_k B_k|.
     Its predicted fp32 error is ε₃₂ times that ratio.
   - Measure `ACECalculator` energy, forces and virial in fp32 against fp64 on
     test configurations.
5. **Speed.** Time row assembly per batch on the A4500 in fp32 and fp64, and
   time the host fp64 QR merge per batch. This shows whether a GPU fp32 → host
   fp64 pipeline would be limited by the rows or by the merge.

**Decision gate:** if the step 3 differences sit well below the training RMSE
and κ of the prior-augmented design stays near 1e8, go to step 3 below (mixed
fit) with the plain host-QR split. Sketching and double-float are needed only
if not.

## Step 2: fp32 inference

- Add a `dtype=` option to `ACECalculator` and `export_lammps`. Set an
  explicit matmul precision on every dot product (`edge_a_kind="matmul"`,
  `block_dense`, PACE `_sbessel_mm`, the readout) so TF32 never applies
  silently.
- Compute `rij` from fp64 positions, then cast it.
- Sum the total energy and the virial in fp64. Use an fp64 readout
  accumulation if step 1.4 shows cancellation.
- Record the cancellation ratio in the bundle and warn above a threshold.
- Tests: an fp32 tier with error-bound tolerances, and an NVE energy-drift
  check.

## Step 3: mixed-precision fit

- fp32 rows on the GPU, cast to fp64 at the merge, with `host_qr_stream`
  merges overlapped with the next batch's rows.
- Targets, weights and residuals stay fp64. Energy rows use a pairwise or
  compensated sum over sites. Virial rows accumulate in fp64.
- Validate the LML, θ_MAP and coefficients against the fp64 fit, and against
  the pipeline goldens with a stated tolerance (not bit-exact).
- GP arm: compute kernel distances from differences, not ‖a‖²+‖b‖²−2a·b. The
  M×M Cholesky stays fp64.
- Radial learning: rows in fp32, the loss reduced in fp64.

## Step 4: only if step 1 says so

- Jacobi column scaling plus prior whitening, before an fp32 QR.
- Sketch-and-precondition: SΦ in fp32 on tensor cores, an fp64 QR of the
  sketch, then LSQR with fp64 accumulation over cached Φ.
- Mixed-precision iterative refinement (or GMRES-IR).
- Double-float merge kernels on the GPU. A test must guard them against XLA
  reassociation.
- Follow cuBLAS fp64 emulation (Ozaki scheme) as it appears in JAX/XLA.
